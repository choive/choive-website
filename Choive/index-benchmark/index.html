What your score means

80–100: Excellent. AI platforms consistently recommend you. You're in the top 18%. Maintain your position by keeping public evidence current.

60–79: Good. AI platforms generally recommend you, but with conditions or alongside competitors. Room to strengthen public evidence.

40–59: Fair. Mixed signals. AI platforms sometimes recommend you, sometimes don't. Focus on evidence gaps and consistency.

0–39: Needs work. AI platforms rarely select you. Systematic evidence gaps. Requires sustained improvement across all platforms.

What drives high scores

Across all verticals, businesses with CHOIVE Scores ≥80 share common patterns:

1. Evidence density

Top performers have 3–5× more public evidence touchpoints: detailed case studies, quantified customer results, transparent pricing, thorough documentation, active community discussions.

2. Specificity over claims

High scorers describe what they do and who it's for in concrete terms. Low scorers rely on adjectives ("leading", "innovative", "best-in-class") without supporting proof.

3. Consistency across platforms

Top businesses maintain aligned positioning, pricing, and messaging everywhere AI looks: website, review sites, community forums, social channels, press.

4. Fresh, current information

Scores correlate with content recency. Businesses updating their public evidence monthly score 14 points higher on average than those updating annually.

5. Buyer-matched questions

High performers answer the questions buyers actually ask. AI platforms select businesses whose public content directly addresses buyer intent and decision criteria.

Score trends over time

As businesses become aware of AI selection dynamics, aggregate scores are rising:

  • Q1 2026: Median score 53, top quartile 69
  • Q2 2026: Median score 56, top quartile 71
  • Q3 2026: Median score 58, top quartile 72 (current)

The bar is rising. A score of 65 was competitive in Q1; now it's table stakes. To maintain relative position, businesses need to improve their public evidence continuously.

How vertical affects platform selection

Not all AI platforms weight evidence the same way across verticals:

  • Developer tools: Claude and ChatGPT favor GitHub activity and documentation quality heavily
  • E-commerce: Perplexity weights product review density and sentiment more than other platforms
  • Professional services: Gemini emphasizes credentials, certifications, and network presence
  • Local services: All platforms converge around Google review count and recency

Your CHOIVE diagnostic breaks this down per-platform, so you know where each AI looks and what it weights.

Using the Index

Compare your score to your vertical's average and top quartile. If you're:

  • Below average: Systematic evidence gaps exist. Run a full diagnostic to see where AI platforms find you weak, then prioritize fixes.
